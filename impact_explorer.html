Why Kurnool / Orvakal Wins at Default Weights (+15.31 Net)

For Hyderabad's high-power AI training workloads (150–400 km radius), the Orvakal Mega Industrial Hub in Kurnool emerges as the highest-scoring non-border candidate. Rather than drawing from Telangana's coal-heavy grid (34.5% RE), batch AI compute co-locates directly at Andhra Pradesh's premier renewable generation corridor.

  • 1,000 MW Kurnool Ultra Mega Solar Park direct interconnection and sub-transmission access.
  • +9.6% State Grid RE Headroom (Andhra Pradesh 44.1% vs Telangana 34.5%).
  • +8.0% Wind Capacity Factor Uplift (Rayalaseema 27.0% vs Southern Telangana 19.0%).
  • Low Water Stress: Dedicated industrial canal allocation from Srisailam reservoir, completely bypassing Hyderabad's urban residential water competition.

The Bengaluru → Tumakuru Water Paradox (ΔRE = 0.0%)

Notice a critical methodological honesty in the model: Tumakuru / Vasanthanarasapura scores +12.83 Net Uplift, yet its RE metrics are completely identical to Bengaluru (61.2% RE, 22.5% Solar, 28.0% Wind). Its uplift is driven 100% by municipal water risk relief.

  • Zero RE Delta (ΔRE = 0%): Karnataka's grid is already maximal; relocating 76 km northwest cannot improve clean power generation.
  • Eliminates Acute Tanker Conflict: Bengaluru's 1,830 ML/yr cooling demand threatens critically depleted urban aquifers.
  • Statutory Industrial Water: Tumakuru secures KIADB statutory Hemavathi river water allocations.
  • Sub-2ms Latency Preserved: 76 km distance ensures sub-2ms network round-trip time, retaining seamless low-latency cloud region connectivity.

Methodology & Mathematical Formulas

Core Project Glossary

1. 24/7 RE-Matching Gap (Temporal Intermittency Deficit)
The percentage of continuous annual data center electricity demand that cannot be met on an hourly basis by un-stored solar and wind generation, which ceases at night or drops in low-wind seasons.
2. Capacity Utilization Factor (CF / CUF)
The ratio of actual electrical energy generated by a renewable asset over a year divided by the theoretical maximum if operating continuously at full nameplate rating.
3. Data Center Utilization Factor (0.65 Standard)
The average continuous facility load ratio accounting for IT server idling, redundancy headroom, and power conditioning losses (calibrated to the 65% industry benchmark).
4. Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE)
The volume of evaporative and facility cooling water consumed per kilowatt-hour of IT electricity (calibrated to the 1.25 L/kWh industry standard).
5. Site Suitability Index (SSI)
A composite multi-criteria decision index evaluating candidate corridors across grid decarbonisation headroom, water aquifer stress, solar/wind resources, and transmission distance.

Model Mathematical Formulas

Electricity Demand Formula
Demand (GWh/yr) = MW × 8,760 hours × Utilization / 1,000
Evaluates continuous annual load under standard 8,760 calendar hours and user-selected utilization factor.
Blended RE Generation Ceiling %
Blended RE % = (Solar_CF × Solar_Weight) + (Wind_CF × Wind_Weight)
Where wind is Not Viable (Delhi-NCR and Kolkata), Blended RE % = 100% × Solar_CF.
24/7 RE Matching Gap %
24/7 Gap % = 1.0 - Blended RE %
Quantifies the continuous un-stored deficit requiring storage firming or grid fallback.
Avoided CO₂ Emissions (t/yr)
CO₂ Avoided = MAX(0, (Demand_GWh × 1,000) × Grid_EF × (Blended_RE - BAU_Grid_RE))
In 8 of 9 clusters, un-stored RE (18.2%–25.2%) sits below the legacy state grid non-fossil baseline (22.4%–61.2%), yielding 0 avoided emissions under standard GHG accounting. Kolkata is the sole positive exception (+7,606 to 12,090 t/yr).
Cooling Water Demand (ML/yr)
Water (ML/yr) = Demand (GWh) × WUE (L/kWh)
1 GWh × 1.25 L/kWh = 1.25 Megalitres (ML). Scales linearly with continuous energy draw.
Site Suitability Uplift & Net Score
Uplift = W_grid·ΔRE + W_water·ΔWater + W_res·(ΔSolar + ΔWind) Net Score = Uplift - W_land × 5.0 × (Distance_km / Radius_Limit_km)
Normalizes multi-criteria candidate attributes against current cluster baselines with distance penalty.
